My Happy Hour

A curated local-discovery product for happy-hour offers in Brentwood/Burnaby and Downtown Vancouver, built around source provenance, verification dates, and maintainable restaurant data.

The problem

Happy-hour data goes stale quickly.

Offers are scattered across websites, PDFs, menus, and location-specific promotions. A useful discovery product therefore needs more than a good interface: it needs a disciplined update and verification workflow.

The approach

Keep source and status visible.

Each restaurant record can carry official links, last-checked dates, verification status, neighbourhood context, offer details, and menu references.

What I built

Local discovery interface

Curated listings

Focused coverage of Brentwood/Burnaby and Downtown Vancouver rather than an unverified city-wide scrape.

Search

Search across restaurants, neighbourhoods, menu highlights, tags, and promotions.

Filters

Open-now, geographic, price, late-night, vegetarian, and patio filters support practical decisions.

Sorting

Sort by opening status, price, and distance to turn the dataset into a useful consumer tool.

Official sources

Map, menu, and official PDF links connect each listing back to source information where available.

Maintainable data

Restaurant records are separated from the interface so they can be checked and updated independently.

Design principle

Discovery is a data-maintenance problem.

The product is designed around the idea that trust depends on knowing where information came from and when it was last checked, especially for promotions that can change without notice.
