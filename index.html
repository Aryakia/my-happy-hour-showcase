My Happy Hour

A curated local-discovery product for happy-hour offers in Brentwood/Burnaby and Downtown Vancouver, designed around source provenance, verification dates, and maintainable restaurant records.

The problem

Happy-hour information has a short shelf life.

Offers are scattered across websites, PDFs, menus, and location-specific promotions. A useful discovery product therefore needs both a strong interface and a disciplined verification workflow.

The design rule

Source, status, and freshness stay visible.

Each record can preserve an official link, last-checked date, verification status, neighbourhood context, offer details, and menu reference. Missing confirmation is marked rather than guessed.

What I built

Decision-oriented local discovery

Focused catalogue

Curated coverage of two target areas instead of a broad unverified city-wide scrape.

Search

Restaurant names, neighbourhoods, menu highlights, tags, and promotions are searchable.

Practical filters

Open-now, location, price, late-night, vegetarian, and patio filters support real decisions.

Sorting + favourites

Sort by opening status, price, or distance and store favourites locally on the device.

Official-source links

Maps, direct menus, and official PDFs remain connected to the record when available.

Maintainable catalogue

Restaurant data is separated from the interface so offers can be rechecked and corrected independently.

Technology

Lightweight web-product architecture.

The working application uses React 19, TypeScript, a Next-compatible App Router, Vite/Vinext-compatible tooling, Cloudflare-compatible output, and automated catalogue/rendered-output tests.

Why it matters

Provenance is a user feature.

For time-sensitive local information, trust depends on knowing where the information came from and when it was last checked. Data quality is not back-office work—it directly determines product usefulness.

Disclosure boundary

Public product story, private working application.

The showcase excludes unpublished maintenance tooling, internal notes, credentials, environment configuration, and private operational data. It exposes only deliberate catalogue-level metrics, product architecture, and verification methodology.
